\subsection{Transport layer justification (R4)}

Reliable transfer & Not needed: a lost ANNOUNCE is replaced 5\,s later. \emph{With TCP:} we must know the peer's address before connecting; finding it is discovery's job. & Needed: chunks must arrive whole and in order. \emph{With UDP:} we would rebuild sequencing, ACKs and retransmission. \\
Throughput & Tiny: $\le$\,512 bytes every 5\,s. \emph{With TCP:} a connection per pair just to say ``I am here''. & Bulk data needs congestion control. \emph{With UDP:} we would write our own, or flood the Wi-Fi. \\
Timing & One broadcast reaches all peers at once. \emph{With TCP:} a separate setup to every peer, and no broadcast. & Sync works in seconds, so retransmission delay is fine. \emph{With UDP:} lower latency we do not need. \\
Security & TLS needs a connection, so each datagram carries an HMAC tag and timestamp. & The HMAC handshake and TLS~1.3 both run over the TCP stream. \emph{With UDP:} TLS could not be used as is. \\

\subsection{Sockets and threads (R2)}
\label{sec:r2}

\textbf{Sockets only (R2).} meshsync uses Python 3.11+ and the \textbf{standard library only}: both
transports use \code{socket} directly, with threads and no networking framework. Format, framing,
handshake and state machine are all ours. Other modules are helpers, not protocols: \code{struct} packs the header, \code{json} encodes bodies,
\code{hashlib} computes SHA-256, \code{hmac} signs tags and handshakes, and \code{ssl} wraps our own
TCP sockets in TLS.

\textbf{Threads.} Each connection has one reader thread and a send lock. Replies are matched to
requests by REQ\_ID, so up to \textbf{8} chunk requests per peer are in flight on one connection.
