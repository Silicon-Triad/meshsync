\section{Sustainability and efficiency analysis}
\label{sec:sustainability}

After a 1\,KiB edit to the 100\,MiB file, meshsync received 171\,836 bytes where a full copy took
105\,183\,306, counting all traffic. That is a 99.84\,\% saving; content-defined chunking gives the same
after an insert (99.85\,\%). We assume, as an illustration, 10\,000 users each making 20 such edits a
day, each sent to one peer: 200\,000 edits a day. Full copies would move 21\,037\,GB a day; delta sync
moves 34\,GB. The saving is about 21\,000\,GB a day, or 7.67\,PB a year. At 0.06\,kWh per GB, the 2015
estimate of Aslan et al.\ \refcite{1}, that is about 1\,260\,kWh a day, or 460\,MWh a year. These are
order-of-magnitude figures. Few real edits touch 100\,MiB files, a three-peer group sends each edit
twice, and the energy figure is from 2015. Against the saving stands CPU work: every peer hashes the
files it scans, and chunking runs a rolling hash over every byte. We did not measure that cost.
